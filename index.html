Reading the vectors

  • Average wind: the prevailing breeze
  • What you feel: average wind + thermal inflow
  • Third vector: B→C, the difference; points at the lift
  • B: where the streamer would lie in the average wind (dashed)
  • Streamer: shows the local wind; longer = stronger. Its tip is C

How the third vector works
  1. Learn the average wind: where each streamer normally sits (point B).
  2. A thermal sucks air in from all sides. Near the ground that inflow adds to the wind, and the streamer moves to C.
  3. The vector from B to C is the third vector, and it points toward the lift.
  4. Wind drops → lift is coming from upwind. Wind picks up → it has passed and is now downwind.
  5. A big or fast-changing shift means the thermal is close. A small, slow one means it's weak or far away.
  6. Streamer goes limp → the thermal is right overhead. Expect the wind to surge back once it has passed.
  7. Two or more streamers give several third vectors. Where they cross is roughly where the thermal is. Streamers downwind of you pick up a thermal that has already passed.
  8. With only your own streamer you get a direction. Judge the distance from how big the shift is and how fast it changes.

Based on Joe Wurts' soaring training notes and Marcus Stent's Thermal Training Notes (2016).
